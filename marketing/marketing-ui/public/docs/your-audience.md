# Your Audience

**Who this is for:** you — a creator on web10. This is the difference
between renting an audience and owning one.

## The list is yours

When someone follows you, they join your **followers group** — and that
group's membership list is **your data**. Not the platform's. Not a number
on a dashboard that nobody can export.

You can read the full list: every follower's web10 username, their email
(if they've set one), their phone (if they've set one). And you can reach
them **directly** — message them, email them, text them. No platform
permission, no "creator tools" gate, no API approval. The relationship
belongs to you.

On the legacy platforms, the equivalent list is the platform's asset:

| | Their platforms | web10 |
|---|---|---|
| Who holds the follower list | The platform | You |
| Can you export it | No (or a CSV that dies in your inbox) | Yes — it's your data |
| Can you message your audience | Through their tools, on their terms | Directly — it's your list |
| What happens if you leave | You leave the audience behind | You take it with you |

That last row is the whole point. Your audience is portable, because it's
a group you own, on a node your data lives on.

## The reach gap (why this matters)

Here's the number that should keep you up at night: on YouTube, a channel
with **1M subscribers** typically gets its next video seen by **~300k of
them**. Google's algorithm decides which slice of your audience sees your
work — and it decides to keep you guessing.

On web10, **100% delivery is architecture.** When you post to your
followers, all of them see it. Not 30%. Not "the algorithm felt like it."
All of them. Every single time. No shadow ban, because there's no shadow —
delivery is a group read, and you can see exactly who's in the group.

This is the pitch in one line: **your audience, no shadow ban.**

## What you can do with the list

- **Reach out directly** — a follower who set an email can be emailed; one
  who set a phone can be texted. A fanbase you can actually talk to.
- **See who's there** — your audience is a readable list, not a black box.
  New followers, lapsed ones, the whole shape of it.
- **Move it** — export it, take it to another node. The list follows you
  because it's yours.
- **Segment it** — close friends is a group. A community is a group. Your
  audience isn't one blob; it's a set of lists you control.

## The controls you hold

Your followers group is yours to run:

- **Public or private** — public profile: anyone follows instantly.
  Private: follows require your approval.
- **Approve and remove** — you decide who's in the list.
- **Post to it** — your posts to followers go to exactly the people in the
  group.

The group's **face** — its name, description, banner — is public by
default (that's the front door: a stranger should be able to see who you
are before they follow), but what's *in* it is access-controlled by your
roles.

## The honest part

The node holds your data, and the node is readable by design — that's the
trade that makes discovery and delivery work, and the node operator is
liable for what they host. Your audience list is your data under your
node's terms, exportable and portable, not a platform asset. If that trade
isn't for you, run your own node — then the list is on your own
infrastructure, full stop.

## Why it's built this way

Follows, close friends, communities, DMs — they're all the same mechanism:
groups. There's no separate "followers table" the platform owns, because
there's no separate followers *anything*. Your followers are a group you
own, with a membership list you can read, and posts that deliver to exactly
its members. One mechanism, and the ownership falls out of it for free.
