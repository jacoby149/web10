# Being a Creator

**Who this is for:** you — a creator on web10. The day-to-day: what you
post, where it goes, how people reach you, and where the controls live.

## Posting

You compose a post — text, a photo, a video — and you choose where it goes.
That choice is the whole visibility model:

| Where | Who sees it |
|---|---|
| **The public board** (the default) | Everyone on the node — your discovery surface |
| **Your followers** | The people who follow you — 100% of them, every time |
| **Close friends** | Your private circle — the people you approved |
| **A community** | That community's members |
| **Nowhere** (no groups) | Just you — a draft, a private note |

A post can go to more than one place. "This is for the board *and* my
close friends" is a real, common post. And you can always pull a post back
— delete it, or move it to fewer groups.

**Delivery is the point.** A post to your followers reaches every follower.
Not a sample, not a slice the algorithm picked — the whole list. That's
how the platform is built, and it's the difference from every feed you've
used before.

## Your groups

You own a small set of groups, and they do the social work:

- **Followers** — your audience. Public (instant follow) or private
  (you approve). This is the list you own — see
  [Your Audience](/docs/your-audience).
- **Close friends** — your inner circle. People request in, you approve.
  Private posts live here.
- **Communities** — groups around a topic. You can create one, join one,
  or both. In a community you can be a member, a moderator, or the owner —
  and moderators can keep things civil (hide content, manage members)
  without owning the place.

There's no "followers tab" or "friends tab" to maintain — a group *is* the
list, and the groups screen in the app is where you see and manage them.

## Your profile

Your profile is your page on the node: your name, your face, your posts.
It's what people see when they tap your name on a post, and it's what they
follow.

- **Public or private** — public: anyone can follow instantly, and a
  signed-out visitor can see your public posts. Private: follows need your
  approval, and only your followers read your follower posts.
- **Your posts** — everything you've shared, grouped by where it went.
- **Your audience** — your followers list, readable and exportable. It's
  your data.

## Engaging with your audience

- **Comments and reactions** live on your posts, from the people in the
  groups that post is shared with. You can reply, and the conversation
  stays on the post.
- **DMs** — a conversation with one person is a private two-person group.
  No inbox to triage: each conversation is its own thing, and it's there
  as long as either of you keeps it.
- **Reach out directly** — a follower who added an email or phone can be
  reached directly. That's the owned-audience part: your list is a list of
  people, not a number.

## The day-to-day, in practice

1. **Make the thing.** Post it to the board (to be found) and/or your
   followers (to be delivered).
2. **Show up.** Reply to comments, work the DMs, approve the follows you
   want.
3. **Tend the lists.** Close friends in and out, community members
   approved, the occasional block (one tap, in the app or the
   authenticator).
4. **Check your numbers.** Your followers count is real — it's the size of
   a group you own, not a metric the platform adjusts.

## Where the controls live

Two surfaces, both yours:

- **The social app** — the day-to-day: compose, feed, profile, messages,
  groups.
- **The authenticator** — the charge: your account (password, phone,
  email), your apps (revoke any of them, or all of them — the kill
  switch), your groups (leave, pause sharing, manage), and your data
  (export, make everything private). See
  [Your Data](/docs/your-data) for the full list.

## The posture

You're not building a following on somebody else's land. You're running a
group on a node, with a list you can read, posts that deliver to exactly
its members, and data you can export. The platform's job is to make that
legible and portable — the audience is yours, and the delivery is
guaranteed by construction, not by a promise.
