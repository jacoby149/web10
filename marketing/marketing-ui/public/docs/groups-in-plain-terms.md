# Groups in Plain Terms

**Who this is for:** you — a web10 user. This explains the social pieces in
everyday language. No jargon, no settings screens.

## The one idea

Everything social on web10 is a **group** — a list of people. When you follow
someone, you join their group. When you post to close friends, you're sharing
with your group. There's no hidden "follows list" or "friends list" somewhere
else — the group *is* the list, and you can always see who's in it.

That's why your audience is yours: the list of your followers is data you
own, not a number the platform controls.

## The five groups you'll actually use

### Follows

When you follow a creator, you join **their followers group**. Instant — no
approval needed for public profiles.

- You see their posts in your feed.
- They can see you're a follower.
- Unfollowing just leaves the group.

Some creators keep their profile **private** — following them sends a request
they have to approve. Same mechanic, one extra step.

### Discover (the public board)

The public board is the group **everyone is already in**. Posts shared there
are visible to every user on the node — signed in or not. It's how new people
find creators, and it's where your posts land by default when you post
publicly.

Posting to the board is how you get discovered. There's no algorithm deciding
who sees your post — everyone does.

### Close Friends

Your private circle. A group you own, where people have to ask to join and
you approve them.

- Posts you share there are visible **only** to your close friends.
- It's the right place for the stuff that isn't for the whole node.
- You control every member — add, remove, any time.

### Communities

A community is a group around a topic — a chess club, a jazz fanbase, a
city. Someone creates it and owns it; members request to join (or are
invited); the owner can add moderators to help keep things civil.

- You can post and comment as a member.
- You can be in as many communities as you like.
- Leaving is one tap.

### DMs (direct messages)

A conversation with one other person is a **two-person group** — just you
two. Messages are posts in that group, and only you two can see them.

No "inbox" to manage: each conversation is its own private group, and it's
there in your message list as long as either of you keeps it.

## Quick reference

| You want to… | Use |
|---|---|
| See what a creator shares | Follow them |
| Be seen by everyone on the node | Post to the public board |
| Share with only your inner circle | Close friends |
| Hang out around a topic | A community |
| Talk one-on-one | A DM |

## Who can see what

- **Public board** → everyone on the node.
- **Followers** → the people who followed (plus everyone, if the profile is
  public).
- **Close friends** → only the members you approved.
- **Community** → only the members.
- **DM** → only the two of you.

And no matter where you post, **you can always pull it back**: delete the
post, leave the group, or stop sharing with a group without leaving it.
That's all in [Your Data](/docs/your-data).
