# Ads

**Who this is for:** you — a creator who wants to earn on web10. This is
what an ad is on this platform, and why it's simpler than the ad you know.

## The one rule

On web10, **an ad is a post with a link that pays.**

Not a campaign. Not a "sponsorship slot." Not an entry in some ad manager
you don't own. It's a piece of content you made — your text, your photo,
your video — plus a link that pays you when someone clicks. That's the
whole object.

There are two parts:

- **The creative** — your content. A photo of the product, a video of you
  using it, a plain text post. It's a post, and it rides the same media
  pipeline as everything else you post.
- **The offer** — the link that pays. Who it's from (the partner), the URL
  itself, the button text (the CTA), and the disclosure line.

And a **status**: `active` or `paused`. Pause it and it stops showing
everywhere it's pinned.

## The offer, in detail

| Part | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Kind** | What kind of link it is | `affiliate` (a program link), `direct` (a brand you cut a deal with), `own_store` (your own merchandise or product) |
| **Partner** | Who it's from | Amazon, Target, your brand |
| **Link** | The one that pays | your tagged affiliate URL, the brand's deal link, your store URL |
| **CTA** | The button text | "Get it," "See my setup," "Shop the look" |
| **Disclosure** | The FTC line | "I may earn a commission from links in this post." |

All three kinds are the same shape. An Amazon tag, a Semrush link, and your
own merch store are each just an offer with a different kind — the platform
doesn't care which program the link came from. It stores the link and
delivers it.

**The platform never rewrites your link, never cloaks it, never inserts its
own.** The creator's link is the link. Your affiliate tag is in it, and it
pays *you*.

## The disclosure is not optional

The FTC line shows on **every ad, always.** It's part of the object, not a
UI option you can turn off. Hiding it is a review rejection.

Say "I may earn a commission" — it's the law, and it's also the trust. The
platforms that hide disclosures are the ones people leave. Yours is up top,
in plain words, on every ad.

## How your followers see it

You make the ad, then you **pin it to a post.** The post carries the ad —
it doesn't copy it. When a follower reads that post, they see the post plus
the ad block under it: the creative, the offer, the CTA, and the
disclosure.

Because delivery is by architecture (a post to your followers reaches all of
them), **your ad reaches 100% of your followers, every time** — not the
slice an algorithm decided "performed well enough." One ad can be pinned to
many posts. Pause the ad and it stops rendering on all of them.

A follower only ever sees ads on posts they can already see — an ad pinned
to a post they can't read simply isn't there. No ad leaks past your
audience.

## Node ads (the other layer)

There's a second ad layer that isn't yours: **node ads.** The node operator
can attach their own ads to posts at a rate they set. You don't manage them,
they're not your money, and they don't replace yours — a post can carry
*your* ad and the *node's* ad at the same time, and each is labeled as to
who made it (yours shows your name; the node's shows the node).

Keep the two straight:

| | Your ads | Node ads |
|---|---|---|
| Who makes them | You | The node operator |
| Whose money | Yours | The node's |
| Who decides | You, per post | The node, at a percentage |
| Labeled as | your name | the node |

You're the channel for your ads. The node is the channel for its own.

## What this is not

- **Not an ad network.** No bidding, no exchange, no third-party targeting.
  The only sponsors a follower sees are the ones *you* chose. (A network
  layer — brands buying inventory, CPM/CPC — is a later, separate thing.
  It's not how you earn here.)
- **Not a payment processor.** web10 doesn't touch your affiliate money.
  The program pays you. See [Payment Rails](/docs/payment-rails).
- **Not memberships or tips.** That's a different surface, a different
  model — also later. This is the *link* side: affiliate, direct deals,
  your own store.

## Where you do this

- **The Ad Catalog** (the authenticator's Studio) — your inventory: every
  ad you've made, its offer, its status, the posts it runs on. See
  [Ad Catalog](/docs/ad-catalog).
- **The composer** (the social app) — make a post, attach an ad from your
  catalog. See [Ad Catalog](/docs/ad-catalog).
- **The ramp** — which programs to join and how to get your first payout.
  See [Affiliate Programs](/docs/affiliate-programs) and
  [Monetization Bootcamp](/docs/monetization-bootcamp).
