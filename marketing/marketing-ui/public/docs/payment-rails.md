# Payment Rails

**Who this is for:** you — a creator who wants to earn on web10. This is
the honest answer to "who gets paid, and who takes a cut."

## The short version

**Your affiliate money never touches the platform.** The program pays you.
Amazon pays Amazon's account — the one with *your* tag. The brand pays the
brand's deal. You are the merchant of record for your own links. web10 is
the **delivery**, not the middleman: it holds the link that pays, wraps it
in your content, and shows it to 100% of your followers. The money flows
from the program straight to you.

That's the whole v3 payment model, and it's deliberate. The platform that
owns your payout can demonetize you with a sentence. The platform that
never touches the payout can't.

## What the platform does (and doesn't) take

| Money | Who pays whom | Platform's cut |
|---|---|---|
| **Your affiliate / direct / store revenue** | The program / brand / your customers → **you** | **None.** It doesn't flow through the platform. |
| **Revenue that flows through web10's rails** (the creator-economy surfaces — memberships and the like, a later model) | Through the platform's payment rails | **~3%** — in the flow of funds, Square-like, not a self-reported license. |

Two things to keep straight:

- **The 3% rail is for money that goes through the rails.** Your affiliate
  payouts don't — the program pays you directly, so there's nothing to
  rail. The 3% applies to the revenue the platform actually processes
  (the creator-economy payment surfaces, which are a later model — see
  below).
- **User accounts are free, always.** End users are never charged. A node's
  economics come from its operator's revenue (the creator economy, the
  node's own ads), not from a subscription fee on the people using it. The
  metering you might see (storage caps, rate limits) is **anti-abuse
  policy set by the operator** — not a bill.

## Why it's built this way

On the paved platforms, your revenue is a black box: the platform's ad
manager owns the inventory, it decides when you "qualify," it takes a cut,
and it can revoke all of it unilaterally. That structure exists because the
platform is in the flow of funds.

web10 removes itself from the flow of funds for the link side:

- **You are the merchant of record** for your own links. Your tag, your
  payout, your terms.
- **The platform can't demonetize you** by switching a flag — there's no
  payout for it to switch. It can moderate content (any platform does); it
  can't claw back a commission you earned.
- **It works at any audience size.** Affiliate and direct deals pay at 100
  followers the same as at 100k. There's no "monetization threshold" to
  unlock, because there's no platform payout to gate.

The trade: you do the program signups yourself, and you're responsible for
your own payouts and taxes. That's the price of being the merchant of
record — and it's the same price every independent creator already pays
anywhere else.

## What's deferred (the later model)

The platform *will* have payment rails — for the surfaces where being in
the flow of funds is the point:

- **Memberships** — fans paying you directly, processed through the
  platform (Stripe Connect). The split on those is its own decision, made
  when that surface ships.
- **Tips** — same family.
- **The ad-network exchange** — brands buying inventory, CPM/CPC, the
  revenue split across platform + creator + partner. A separate, later
  layer. It's not how you earn in v3; it's not a replacement for your
  links.

Until those ship, the money you can make on web10 is the link side:
affiliate, direct deals, and your own store. That's not a limitation —
it's the part that works today, at any audience size, with the platform
taking nothing.

## The posture, in one line

**The link pays you; web10 just delivers it.** Your revenue is yours from
the moment the program cuts the check — the platform's role is getting your
content in front of 100% of your audience, and it's paid for by the node's
own economics, not by a cut of yours.
