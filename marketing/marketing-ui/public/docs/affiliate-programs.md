# Affiliate Programs

**Who this is for:** you — a creator who wants to earn on web10. This is
the first half of making money: getting into the programs whose links pay
you.

## The shortlist

These are the programs worth joining first — real catalogs, real payouts,
and a low enough bar that a mid-tier creator can get in and earn in the
first month. The Affiliate Programs card in the Studio (the "START HERE"
card) lists each one with a sign-up link.

| Program | Niche | Commission | Why it's worth it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amazon Associates** | Universal e-commerce | 1–10% per sale | The catalog is everything. Lowest bar to start; the tag is the whole setup. |
| **Walmart Creator** | Retail, grocery, electronics | Up to 4% | Physical-retail trust, strong conversion on everyday items. |
| **Target Partners** | Lifestyle, apparel, home | Up to 8% | 7-day cookie (vs Amazon's 24h) — more credit for the click. |
| **eBay Partner Network** | Used, vintage, refurbished | 1–4% | Inventory you can't buy elsewhere; great for a "finds" niche. |
| **TikTok Shop Affiliate** | Viral, social-first products | 10–30%+ (volatile) | Native in-app checkout; the highest ceiling, the least stable. |
| **Shopify Affiliate** | E-commerce software, business tools | Up to 200% of monthly plan | High-value flat payouts for business/creator-economy traffic. |
| **Fiverr Affiliates** | Freelance, digital services | $15–$150 per signup | Dozens of service categories; fits a "how I run my business" angle. |
| **Semrush Affiliate** | SEO, marketing, SaaS | $200/sale + $10/trial | 120-day cookie; high-intent digital traffic converts. |
| **HubSpot Affiliate** | B2B software, CRM | 30% recurring for a year | Sticky software = predictable recurring payouts. |

**This is a map, not a contract.** Commission rates and cookie windows
shift. Confirm on the program's own page before you pitch a brand off the
numbers. And it's a shortlist, not a fence — if a program fits your niche
and isn't listed, it still works: on web10, every one of these is the same
primitive (an offer with a link), so a new program is just a new offer.

## Pick your lane before you sign up

Don't join everything. Pick the 1–2 programs that match what you actually
post about:

- **A tech creator** — Amazon (gear) + Semrush or Shopify (tools).
- **A lifestyle creator** — Amazon + Target + eBay.
- **A service creator** — Fiverr + HubSpot.

The match between your content and the catalog is what makes the link feel
genuine instead of slathered on.

## The sign-up ramp (the part that trips people up)

Each program has its own signup. Three things are common to all of them:

**1. You need a site to list.** Programs like Amazon Associates ask for the
"top-level websites and/or mobile apps where you'll display links." On
web10, that's your node's domain — `web10.app`, `social.web10.app`, or your
custom domain if you have one. You don't need to enumerate every path or
subdomain; the registered domain is the property. List the bare domains.

**2. You need to actually display the links.** Amazon's **180-day rule**: if
your listed site doesn't show a live, tagged link within 180 days, the
account closes. So the signup and the first ad are the *same task* — sign
up, then make the first ad in the ad maker the same week. (See
[Ad Catalog](/docs/ad-catalog).)

**3. The tag is yours.** Your Associates tag (e.g. `mysite-20`) is the
credential. It goes in the offer's link — and it pays *you*, not the node.
Treat it like a revenue identity: keep it yours.

## The node account vs. your account

If you also run the node (you're the operator, not just a creator on it),
that's a **separate** affiliate account for the **node's** ads. Your
creator account is for **your** ads.

Two accounts can list the same domain — the programs key payment off the
**tag in the link**, not off which account listed the site. But don't funnel
your creator revenue through the node's account: that makes the node the
merchant of record for your sales, which is a payments-company problem the
platform deliberately doesn't want. Your link, your tag, your payout —
separate from the node's.

You have a link that pays. The next step is making it a post: the ad maker
