# The Touch-Up

Not everything needs a rewrite. Sometimes a doc is mostly right but has a logical error, an inconsistency, or a small thing that's just off. The touch-up is for those moments — surgical, minimal, no fanfare.

## When to Use

- A factual error: wrong version number, wrong file path, outdated API endpoint
- An inconsistency: one section says "MongoDB," another says "FerretDB," and they mean the same thing
- A logical gap: a step is missing in a flow, a variable is referenced before it's defined
- A broken link or dead reference

## How It Works

1. Find the error.
2. Fix the error.
3. Stop.

The touch-up is not a reason to rewrite a paragraph, restructure a section, or polish prose. It's a one-line fix, a corrected word, a patched reference. If the fix touches more than a few lines, you're not doing a touch-up — you need a bigger editing style.

## The Discipline

The temptation is to start fixing things around the error. "While I'm here..." No. The touch-up is scoped to the specific problem. Other issues can wait for their own pass.

## When Not to Use

- The doc is structurally broken — needs restructuring
- The doc is bloated — needs aggressive cutting
- The doc is flat and reads poorly — needs surface polish
- The doc targets the wrong audience — needs rewriting

Those are bigger problems. A touch-up is for when the doc is fundamentally sound and just has a flaw.
