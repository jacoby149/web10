\subsubsection{Million-account sizing arithmetic and the load preflight}
\label{sec:budget}

The realism scenario proposes 0.25 page reads per account per day, or
250,000 per day at one million accounts (about 2.9\,s$^{-1}$ on average).
The 15\,s$^{-1}$ peak is a declared planning input, not a measured traffic
peak or a claim about the communities used to fit authorship.

At that peak the measured indexed and hybrid per-read logical totals imply
1.85 and 1.39 decimal GB/s respectively. The latter replaces 0.54\,GB/s,
which was a transfer of the transient slice ratio, not a full-store result.
Logical bytes are neither a bound on nor a measure of physical disk bytes.
The reference R630 has four 2\,TB SATA SSDs in two mirrored pairs; the
vendor rates each at 545\,MB/s sequential reads \cite{sandisk}. Depending
on mirror behavior the gross rating is 1.09 or at most 2.18\,GB/s, not net
supply alongside writes and merges. No physical disk-budget pass follows
from comparing these quantities. Neither logical demand nor the October
production browser observation measures that array's physical supply.

Statement peaks against an approximately 17\,GB per-read planning allowance
do not bound overlapping queries, writers, caches, or merges. The product's
500\,ms complete HTTP line remains unmet. Summed native durations, including
1,332\,ms in the indexed archive, are request statement elapsed times, not
CPU times or bounds on multithreaded CPU. Neither the CPU budget nor a
concurrent latency distribution was verified.

The separate sustained-load contract specified 150 distinct prepared reader
tokens with the same K20 request and authority, 15 reads/s at peak, 2.9
reads/s at average, and one publication/s in the load windows. Its target
host was 16 cores, 32 threads, and 32\,GB of RAM. The closest available
32\,GB dedicated-vCPU SKU exposed 16 vCPUs with one thread per visible
core; it did not supply an observed 32-thread operating point. The
Parquet restore from the verified frozen input reconciled 88,200,300
documents, 92,200,600 links, and 1.6 billion events. Restore and hot-table
preparation are not workload evidence.

The membership census found only one principal with all 21 groups in the
banked request (20 followed groups and the original reader's own group),
and no public or authenticated group rows that could authorize other
readers. The preflight therefore refused the contracted pool before
calibration. No grants or reader identities were substituted. Warm-up,
peak, average, drain, and their latency and debt measurements did not run.
This is a fixture authority finding, not a bound on sustainable throughput
or a verdict on the host's resource envelope (\pr{1706}).
