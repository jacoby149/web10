# The look: figures that look like a research paper

Research papers have a look. Ours:

- **Diagrams:** the HTML→PNG@2x system
  (`../../second-draft/figs/README.md`) — committed sources, one
  consistent dark web10 theme, the same mechanism drawn identically
  everywhere, every figure referenced from the body and
  caption-accurate. A figure enters the paper **only through
  `../../second-draft/kb/diagrams.md`** — what it visualizes, why that
  shape, why it is visually nice. The design language (semantic color:
  green = the trust source, violet = the enforcement mechanism, amber =
  the negative space, red = the broken path; solid = primary flow,
  dashed = cross-boundary; chip = data object, card = node, pill = the
  invariant being claimed) is pinned there.
- **Plots:** the familiar shapes — bar, line, CDF, grouped. One
  question per plot; the question is the first sentence of the caption.
- **The figure that maps the paper:** the overview figure carries
  section labels (§2, §4, §5, …) — a visual table of contents (the F3
  move). The reader can look at Figure 1 and know the paper's structure.
- **Consistency:** one palette, one font, one figure grammar. A
  reviewer's eye should see a system, not a pile. The dark theme doubles
  as a Q1 signal: the figures and the UI screenshots share one visual
  world, and that world is the product.

The diagram shots are a gate in the shot loop
(`../method/README.md`): the visual spine is approved before the prose
writes around it.
