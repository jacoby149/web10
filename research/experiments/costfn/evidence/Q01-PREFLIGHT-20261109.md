## What the workload represents

**K=1,000 is a high-follow stress case, not an established average.** Operator:
“1000 is crazy i think, 20 groups more likely” and “do you think people will be
in 1000 groups? i.e. that is a realistic average?” No observed web10 distribution
establishes either 20 or 1,000 as the average.

Each synthetic reader has 1,000 creator followers-group memberships plus
Discover: **1,001 seeded memberships**. The main query requests the 1,000
followers groups. Creator accounts have their own followers group plus Discover.
The input contains 1M creators and 20k readers; this acceptance uses **two reader
sessions**, not 20k concurrent users. They produce six overlapping feed tasks
and a peak of eight HTTP requests, with zero after drain. A group here is often
a creator's distribution/access group, not a chat room.

Thus this establishes a limit under heavy-follow conditions on a large node;
it is not evidence that two ordinary users with about 20 groups cannot be served.
Conversely, fewer requested groups may not remove the broad document scans.
A K=20 scenario is a reasonable next candidate to design/test, not a predicted
pass or measured-average claim. It needs a separately identified recipe, real
app request/output/paging checks and explicit disclosure of any changed post/
membership cardinalities. Never relabel this K=1,000 receipt, silently sample its
groups, overwrite frozen inputs or launch another paid run without approval.
